### Six names, four ideas

The sector cap passes on paper — Industrials 2/6, Healthcare 2/6, both at the
one-third limit. But the count flatters the list. **VRT and BE are the same
bet**: power infrastructure for AI data centres. **TMO and A are the same bet**:
life-sciences instruments. Treat this as four independent ideas with two of them
expressed twice, not as six.

### 1. TRU — TransUnion · $84.94 (2026-09-03) · $16.28B · P/E 21.7

Surfaced by the catalyst angle and the cleanest fit to the stated frame of
anything found in two runs. Q2 2026 beat and full-year guidance raised, at a
P/E of 21.73 with a small dividend (0.59% yield). A credit bureau is a
recurring-revenue data business, not a cyclical — and it is the only name here
whose multiple would not raise an eyebrow in a value screen.

What breaks it: credit bureaus earn on lending volume, and lending volume falls
when rates rise. With a September hike live and inflation at a three-year high,
the macro that makes the multiple attractive is the same macro that could take
the earnings down. This is the name where the rate call matters most directly.

### 2. VRT — Vertiv Holdings · $279.80 (2026-09-08) · $108B · P/E 63.5

**Not VIRT.** Vertiv makes power and thermal management systems for data
centres — the picks-and-shovels layer of the AI buildout, sitting in
industrials rather than technology. The catalyst is specific and recent: a
**$1.45B acquisition of Utility Innovation Group** alongside **raised full-year
2026 adjusted earnings guidance**. Buying capability and raising the outlook in
the same announcement is a stronger signal than either alone.

What breaks it: 63.5x earnings, and at $108B this is not an undiscovered name —
there is a real chance it is already on the user's ticker list, which would make
it a wasted slot exactly as the mega-caps were yesterday. It is also the
purest expression of the AI-capex cycle on this list, so it carries that
cycle's timing risk.

### 3. ESTC — Elastic N.V. · $89.20 (2026-09-08) · $9.37B · P/E 25.7

**Yesterday's verification failure, now resolved.** The 2026-09-07 run found the
catalyst — shares jumped over 17% after full-year guidance topped expectations
— but could not source a price or market cap, so the rule excluded it. Both are
now available and dated (traded $88.10–$91.35 on 2026-09-08).

The number that makes it interesting is the P/E of 25.71. Against a software
sector that de-rated to a ~3.8x median EV/Sales in 2026 and now trades at a
discount to the S&P 500, a profitable search-and-observability company at 25.7x
earnings with a raised guide is a different proposition from the high-multiple
names that de-rating punished.

What breaks it: the same macro that threatens all software into a live hike,
and a smaller balance sheet than the others here at $9.37B.

### 4. BE — Bloom Energy · $252.87 (2026-09-08) · $74.48B · P/E 344.6

**Outside the stated style, and included anyway — deliberately.** A P/E of
344.56 is not a balanced-style holding by any reading, and this report is not
pretending otherwise.

It is here because of one dated, mechanical fact: **Bloom Energy joins the S&P
500 on 2026-09-21**, inside the one-month horizon. Index inclusion forces
passive funds to buy regardless of valuation, and that is a different kind of
catalyst from an earnings beat — it does not depend on anyone's judgement being
right. Underneath it sits a record Q2 and a significant upward revision to
full-year 2026 revenue guidance, plus the same data-centre power theme as VRT.

What breaks it: 344x earnings means the inclusion flow is very likely already
anticipated and partly priced — index events are the most telegraphed catalysts
in the market. If the flow is in the price, what remains is a fuel-cell company
at 344x into a rate hike. Whoever owns this is making an event trade, not an
investment, and should size it that way.

### 5. TMO — Thermo Fisher Scientific · $618.43 · ~$214.58B (2026-08-03)

The healthcare thread that yesterday's run surfaced and did not follow.
Morningstar's position: healthcare has underperformed the broad market in 2026
with undervalued opportunities across biopharma, medical devices and
distributors — with the important qualifier that **Eli Lilly is roughly 10% of
US healthcare market cap and is seen as overvalued**, so the sector looks
better once it is excluded. TMO sells scientific instruments, lab equipment,
diagnostic consumables and life-science reagents into that market.

What breaks it: this is a **sector thesis, not a name-level catalyst** — nothing
specific to TMO was found, which is weaker footing than the four names above.
And its verification is the shakiest here: the price carried no date and the
market cap is dated 2026-08-03. The two are mutually consistent (implying ~347M
shares), which is why it survives, but it is ranked fifth for that reason.

### 6. A — Agilent Technologies · $153.84 (undated) · $43.38B · P/E 30.3

What breaks it: identical to TMO — no name-specific catalyst was found and the
price carries no as-of date. Ranked last on evidence quality, not on quality of
