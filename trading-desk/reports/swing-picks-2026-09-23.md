## One risk that applies to all three, stated once

**All three report after the close.** A stop placed under structure protects
against *drift before the print*. **It does not protect across the print** —
an earnings gap opens the next morning wherever it opens, and a stop 3% away is
filled 10% away if that is where the stock opens. The "risk to stop" figures
below are pre-event risk only. The real event risk is the overnight move, and
no implied-move figure could be sourced for any of the three.

## Findings

**The Wait verdict and a swing list fit together better than expected.** Dated
catalysts five and six sessions out mean there is no need to act today; the
calendar supplies the patience the verdict asks for. The one exception, Costco,
is also the weakest candidate — which may not be coincidence, since a catalyst
tomorrow leaves no room to wait for a better tape.

**A handoff timing gap between skills.** Day-picks handed KBH to swing-picks
yesterday as a good candidate; by the time swing-picks ran, KBH's event had
passed. **A cross-skill handoff should carry the catalyst's date**, and the
receiving skill should check it first.

**A retry resolved, and it corrected its own original diagnosis.** NKE's 09-10
failure was a wrong share count (1.20B vs 1.481B), not the stale cap that report
blamed. The cross-check caught the bad data both times — it just cannot say
*which* input was bad. **When a cross-check fails, check the share count first**:
it is the input most often wrong, and a cap tends to lag by days, not by 27%.

**Search bias**: all three survivors are **earnings** catalysts. The FDA angle
produced nothing and the contrarian angle produced names without dates. This is
not a market observation — it is that earnings dates are the only catalyst type
this tooling can reliably confirm.
