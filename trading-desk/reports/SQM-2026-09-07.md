**Executive Summary**: No new position at $76.43. Existing holders keep it, but

**Investment Thesis**: This is the third Hold from this desk in four runs and
the third *distinct kind* of Hold, which is worth stating so the rating is not
read as a pattern. INTC was balanced evidence. VIRT was a right idea with an
entry that could not be risk-managed. SQM is different again: **excellent
earnings that substantially accrue to someone other than the shareholder.**

The Q2 2026 print (2026-08-19) was exceptional and is not in dispute. EPS $2.31
against $1.91 consensus, revenue $2.47B beating by $280M, **adjusted EBITDA
more than quadrupled year on year**, record lithium sales volumes, and lithium
and derivatives revenue of $1,779.2M — **up 299.7%**. First-half profit was
$1.02B. Capital returns are improving: a special dividend was paid and
management proposed lifting the final payout ratio from 30% to 50% of net
income.

Two structural facts survive that, and the debate turned on them. First, the
Codelco joint venture — **NovaAndino Litio**, 50:50, carrying a **golden share
for Codelco that hands it control of Atacama operations from January 2031**.
SQM did receive something concrete for it: a quota increase of **300,000 tonnes
LCE by end-2030**. But the trade was volume now for control later, and it caps
what the equity can ever be worth on a long horizon. Second, the Chilean state's
participation: over **US$1.6B accrued in H1 2026** in corporate and mining
taxes plus the dividend accrued to Codelco.

The risk review materially corrected the desk on that second point, and the
correction is kept rather than buried: **$1.6B of gross payments is not
comparable to $1.02B of net profit**, because it includes corporate taxes every
profitable miner pays out of pre-tax income. Setting the two side by side, as
the bear did, double-counts. What survives the correction is narrower and still
real — the Codelco dividend and the 2031 golden share are claims an ordinary
taxpayer does not face.

What keeps this from Underweight is that reducing requires believing the cycle
is rolling over, and the evidence for that is thin: one report that "lithium
gains cool" and delayed expansion ramps flagged on the call, against a
quadrupled EBITDA and quota-backed volume growth to 2030. What keeps it from
Overweight is that a commodity producer at a cyclical peak, whose upside is
structurally shared, is not a name to build into at 88% above its 52-week low
with no usable technical level to manage against.

**Price Target**: not set. Consensus across 18 analysts (S&P Global) is **Buy**
with an average target of **$84.78** — roughly +10.9% from spot. Individual
targets are unusually dispersed: Scotiabank raised to **$104** (+36%),
JPMorgan lowered to **$94** (+23%), with a published range of **$44.35 to
$110** — a spread of more than 85% of the share price, which is what a market
looks like when it has no shared view of where a commodity cycle ends.

**Time Horizon**: 3–6 months
