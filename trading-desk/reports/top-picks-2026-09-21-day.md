## Findings

**The intraday data constraint is confirmed unchanged, ten days on.** This is
now the second measurement rather than a single observation: every movers feed
blocked, on two separate days, with the same proxy behaviour. The 09-11
conclusion should be treated as settled rather than provisional, and future
`day`-horizon runs should skip the feed attempts entirely and go straight to
news-based sourcing, which is the only channel that has ever produced names.

**Mid-session beats the open for this workflow.** Written three hours in rather
than twenty-two minutes in, the same method produced two names cross-checking
inside 0.02% instead of one loose and one unpinnable. If a `day` run is
requested again, run it mid-session.

**Monday changes the angle.** Weekend deal and partnership news produced the
day's biggest mover; earnings produced nothing. The catalyst angle is
day-of-week dependent, which no previous run had noticed.

**Two capitalisation figures were discarded rather than reconciled** — ACN's
$121.07B and COIN's $51.25B — and in both cases the cross-check is what caught
them. Combined with the two currency-inconsistent caps caught this morning,
that is **four bad capitalisations in two runs on one day**, all caught by the
same rule. The market-cap cross-check instituted after the SQM error on 09-07
has now justified itself more than any other rule the desk has.

**Search bias**: the day's movers cluster in AI infrastructure and crypto,
which is the same risk-on tape rather than independent ideas. The decliners
(NVO, HPQ, UPS, TLX) are unconnected to each other and to that theme, and it is
notable that **not one of them could be verified** while both risk-on names
verified cleanly — a reminder that what gets *reported with numbers* is not a
random sample of what moved.
