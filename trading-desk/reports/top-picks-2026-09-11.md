the result.

## Why this horizon breaks the funnel

This skill exists to feed two things: the user's app, which computes relative
strength and a fundamental score, and then `/trading-desk`. At a `day` horizon
both handoffs fail, and so does the sourcing.

**1 · The app cannot score these.** Its RS is percentile-ranked from **a year of
daily bars**. That is a multi-week momentum measure by construction. It has no
intraday input and cannot distinguish a stock 30 minutes into an 11% range day
from one drifting sideways.

**2 · `/trading-desk` is the wrong instrument.** It runs four analysts, a
bull-bear debate, a three-way risk review, and produces a 3–6 month thesis over
many minutes. Pointing that at a day trade is a category error — by the time it
ruled, the session would be over.

**3 · The data a day trade actually needs was unobtainable.** Every intraday
feed was blocked by the network proxy — TradingView, Benzinga, CNBC, CNN,
MarketChameleon, StockAnalysis, TradingEconomics. What could not be sourced for
any candidate:

- **Relative volume** — the primary gap-and-go filter. Without it there is no
  way to tell a real move from a quiet drift.
- **Real-time price.** Every figure above is news-derived with unknown delay.
  At a 1-month horizon a stale price costs a fraction of a percent of the
  thesis; at a 1-day horizon it can be the entire edge.
- Opening range, VWAP, bid-ask spread, premarket volume, short interest.

**4 · The report outlives the setup.** ORCL has already traversed $17 today.
A shortlist assembled over the time this one took describes a tape that has
moved on.

The honest conclusion: **the `1m` horizon is the one this funnel is built for.**
These two names are real and dated, and they are better used as *swing*
candidates — where the verification actually holds — than as intraday setups.

## Search bias to report

**The catalyst angle dominated this run** — six of ten raw candidates were
earnings reactions. That is not a market observation, it is what a `day`
horizon does to the sourcing: earnings gaps are the most *reported* intraday
events, so they are the ones a news-based search can see at all. Screen-based
angles (relative volume, gappers, unusual options) returned nothing usable
because every feed serving them is blocked.

So the list is biased toward **stocks that moved for a reportable reason**, and
silent on stocks moving on flow. At a day horizon that is a serious blind spot,
and it is a second reason to treat this run's output as swing candidates.

**Verification remains the binding constraint** — third consecutive run where
that is true, and at this horizon it is more binding, not less. Of ten raw
candidates, **eight were killed on price**, all but one for the same reason: a
percentage move was reportable and a price level was not.

**Watchlist and portfolio unavailable for a fifth consecutive run** —
`mitp_watchlist_v1` and `mitp_portfolio_v1` could not be read, so exclusions
were applied only against `trading-desk/decision-log.md`.
