# ORCL — Oracle Corporation
**Analysis date**: 2026-09-11 · **Depth**: standard

## Decision
**Rating**: Hold

**Executive Summary**: No action. Oracle grew revenue ~30% to $19.35B, beat on both lines, grew cloud infrastructure **121%** to $7.4B, and trades at roughly **20x** its own FY27 EPS guidance — which on the income statement alone would be an easy Overweight. Both decisive metrics were fetched before the debate, as the extended rule requires, and **both came back negative**: sequential RPO additions collapsed from **+$85B to +$26B (−69%)**, and the balance sheet financing that growth is at **BBB−, one notch above junk**, with negative free cash flow and no timetable to turn it positive. That is the BKE pattern — both metrics negative — and it produces the same answer for the same reason. No stop is proposed: the price could not be pinned tighter than a **$157–165 band** on a day with a $17 range.

**Investment Thesis**: The income statement and the cash flow statement describe two different companies, and the equity is a claim on both. On the income statement Oracle is a hypergrowth business at a value multiple: revenue ~+30% to **$19.35B** against $19.14B expected, non-GAAP EPS **$1.92** against $1.74, cloud revenue **+62% to $11.61B**, OCI **+121% to $7.4B**, GPU utilisation reported at **97.9%**, and FY27 guidance of at least **$90B** revenue (+34% constant currency) with **$8.10** of non-GAAP EPS — about **20x forward** at today's price. On the cash flow statement it is a leveraged infrastructure builder: **$28.5B of capex in a single quarter against $19.35B of revenue**, FY27 capex guided to **$90–95B against revenue of "at least $90B"** — roughly **103% of revenue** — FY26 free cash flow of **−$23.7B**, Q1 FY27 free cash flow of about **−$5.5B**, and a CFO who **gave no timetable for turning it positive**.

What decides the rating is that the two metrics this desk names in advance both failed, and they failed in a way that compounds. The bull narrative on Oracle is entirely RPO: a $664B backlog, 7.4 years of revenue, up $209B year over year. But the *rate of change* has broken. Sequential additions went **$85B in Q4 FY26 to $26B in Q1 FY27**, a 69% deceleration, at the precise moment capex commitments hit their maximum. Capex is contracted; bookings are not. And **about half of that $664B backlog is owed by OpenAI** — a private company that has never generated a profit, which S&P explicitly named a "key credit risk" when cutting Oracle to **BBB−**, flagging a further downgrade if leverage stays above 4.5x. Debt-to-equity is **500%** and net debt is around **$131B**. Oracle plans to raise **~$40B in FY27 including a potential $20B equity offering (~4.8% dilution)** — and the reporting is explicit that equity is being used *because more debt would likely trigger another downgrade*. That is a constrained balance sheet, not a chosen capital structure.

This desk held VICI three days ago on tenant concentration — ~70% of rent from two tenants, one sub-investment-grade — and on "a refinancing test nobody here can handicap." Oracle is worse on both axes: a single counterparty behind roughly half the backlog, the company itself one notch above junk, and free cash flow negative with no stated path back. What Oracle has that VICI did not is genuine hypergrowth, and that is why this is a Hold rather than an Underweight. Nothing is deteriorating operationally; the risk is structural and unfunded, and at ~20x forward it is not compensated enough to add exposure.

**Price Target**: Not set, and the reason is itself the most informative fact in the file. The consensus is **$241.43** against a price near $160 — roughly **+48%** — and for once this desk can *use* an aggregate, because four named dated actions corroborate the level: **JPMorgan (Mark Murphy) $185→$210 Hold**, **Scotiabank $241→$215 (lowered)**, **Morgan Stanley (Keith Weiss) $175→$246 Hold**, **Barclays (Raimo Lenschow) $221→$281 Buy**. But the panel's high estimate is **$400** and its low is **$110** — a **3.6x spread** on a $441B company. That is the widest dispersion this desk has encountered, and it is not noise: it is the market openly conceding it cannot value this. Note also that two of the three large raises came attached to **Hold** ratings — targets went up while conviction did not.

**Time Horizon**: Event-governed. Q2 FY27 results in December are the first test of whether sequential RPO additions re-accelerate off $26B. The equity offering, whenever it prices, is the dilution event. Neither is a price level, so neither is tradeable now.

### Fundamentals
Two statements, both true, and the gap between them is the investment case.

**Statement one: this is one of the fastest-growing large businesses in the world, at a modest multiple.** Revenue rose ~30% to **$19.35B**, beating. Non-GAAP EPS of **$1.92** beat $1.74 by 10%. Cloud revenue grew **62% to $11.61B**; **OCI grew 121% to $7.4B**. Management guides FY27 to **at least $90B of revenue, +34% constant currency**, and **$8.10** of non-GAAP EPS. At a price near $160 that is **roughly 20x forward earnings** (19.4x at $157.14, 20.4x at $165) and **4.9x forward sales**. For 34% growth, a PEG well below 1. Reported GPU utilisation of **97.9%** says the capacity being built is being consumed, not stranded.

**Statement two: the company is spending slightly more than its entire revenue on capital expenditure, funded with debt it can no longer comfortably raise.** Capex was **$28.5B in the quarter against $19.35B of revenue** — 147% of it. FY27 capex is guided to **$90–95B against revenue of at least $90B**, about **103%**. FY26 free cash flow was **−$23.7B** (operating cash flow $32B less capex $55.7B — the cross-check is exact). Q1 FY27 free cash flow was about **−$5.5B**, and the **CFO gave no timetable for positive free cash flow**.

The balance sheet has already registered this. **S&P cut Oracle to BBB− in July 2026, one notch above speculative grade**, with a further downgrade flagged if leverage stays above 4.5x. **Debt-to-equity is about 500%**; net debt is roughly **$131B**; EBIT coverage ~5.9x. And the financing plan is the tell: **~$40B to be raised in FY27 including a potential $20B equity issue (~4.8% dilution)**, reportedly using equity *because more debt would likely trigger another downgrade*. Equity issued from necessity rather than opportunity is a different signal from equity issued at a high price.

One genuine mitigant deserves weight: the guide distinguishes **$90–95B of gross capex from "no more than $70B of net cash capex."** A $20–25B gap implies leases, vendor or partner financing carrying part of the build. That materially softens the cash burn, and neither the headline capex number nor the FCF number captures it. It also means the true obligation is less visible than the reported one, which cuts both ways.

**The record $23B of operating cash flow needs its own sentence**, because it is attributed partly to "collection of customer prepayments." Prepayments are customer cash received ahead of delivery — real money, but it is working capital, not earnings, and it is borrowed from future quarters' cash flow. A record operating cash flow number that leans on prepayments while free cash flow is negative is the same class of composition question as BKE's tariff refund, and it is flagged as such rather than resolved, because the split was not disclosed.

RPO reached **$664B**, up **$209B** year over year and **$26B** sequentially — against a **$85B sequential rise the prior quarter**. ## Researcher debate

### Bull — opening
Start with what Oracle actually did this quarter, because the bear is going to want to talk about the balance sheet and not the business.

Revenue **+30% to $19.35B**, a beat. Non-GAAP EPS **$1.92** against $1.74 — a **10% beat**. Cloud revenue **+62% to $11.61B**, a beat. And **OCI +121% to $7.4B**. A $441B company grew its infrastructure business at triple digits. Guidance for the full year is **at least $90B of revenue, +34% in constant currency**, and **$8.10** of EPS.

Now price it. At roughly $160 that is **about 20 times forward earnings**. Twenty times, for 34% revenue growth. Name me another mega-cap growing at that rate on that multiple — there isn't one, and the reason is the bear's argument, which is already in the price. The stock is **down 54% from its 52-week high**. The de-rating has happened. What the bear wants to warn me about, the market has already charged me for.

And the demand is not speculative. **GPU utilisation is 97.9%.** The capacity Oracle has built is full. This is not a company constructing empty data centres on hope; it is one that cannot build fast enough, which is precisely why capex looks the way it does. Capex of $28.5B against $19.35B of revenue is what it looks like when you are supply-constrained in the largest infrastructure build of the decade — spending ahead of revenue is the *point*, not a defect.

Finally, the backlog: **$664B, up $209B year over year**, 7.4 years of revenue, with about half converting over 36 months — roughly **$111B a year against a $90B guide**. And **$30B of new AI contracts booked this quarter alone**, with the **non-OpenAI backlog more than doubling** over the past year.

### Bear — reply
I'll take the bull's own headline number and turn it over, because he quoted the level and skipped the derivative.

**RPO went from $638B to $664B. That is +$26B. The prior quarter it was +$85B.** Sequential bookings decelerated **69%** in one quarter. The bull's entire thesis is that this backlog compounds — 7.4 years of revenue, half converting in 36 months — and the compounding just broke. He quoted "+$209B year over year," which is a trailing number that includes the quarter when it was still working.

Now put that next to the spending, because the timing is the whole problem. **Capex was $28.5B in the quarter against $19.35B of revenue** — 147% — and FY27 capex is guided to **$90–95B against revenue of "at least $90B."** Oracle is committing to spend **103% of its revenue** on capacity in the same quarter its bookings growth fell by more than two-thirds. **Capex is contracted. Bookings are not.** If $26B is the new run rate rather than a lumpy quarter, Oracle has built for a demand curve that stopped bending upward, while carrying the debt to pay for it.

And the debt is not theoretical. **S&P cut Oracle to BBB− in July — one notch above junk** — and said another cut follows if leverage stays above 4.5x. **Debt-to-equity is 500%.** Net debt is about **$131B**. Free cash flow was **−$23.7B** in FY26 and about **−$5.5B** this quarter, and the CFO **would not give a timetable** for turning it positive. That is not a growth company choosing to invest; that is a company whose CFO cannot tell you when the cash burn ends.

Then the part the bull mentioned only to dismiss: **roughly half of that $664B backlog is OpenAI.** A private company that has never earned a profit and burns cash. **S&P named it as a key credit risk in the downgrade.** Oracle is borrowing at BBB− to build capacity for a counterparty that may not be able to pay for it.

### Bull — second exchange
The RPO deceleration is the bear's best point and I'm not going to wave it away — one quarter at +$26B after +$85B is a real datum and it deserves the next print to resolve it. But he is reading a lumpy series as a trend. RPO moves in enormous discrete contracts; a single OpenAI-scale signing lands in one quarter and nothing lands the next. **$85B was not a run rate either.** If the bear gets to annualise $26B into a "new normal," he must explain why he did not annualise $85B into $340B a year ago — he would have been just as wrong in the other direction. And note what he skipped: **more than $30B of new AI cloud contracts booked in the quarter**, which is *more* than the $26B net RPO change, because revenue recognition draws the balance down as bookings add to it.

On the counterparty point, he is arguing against the wrong quarter. The single most important thing management said is that the **non-OpenAI backlog more than doubled over the past year**, with Meta and xAI named. The concentration he is describing is the *legacy* shape of the book, and it is diluting — which is exactly what you would want to see, and it is happening.

And on the balance sheet: he keeps saying BBB− as though it were a default. It is **investment grade**. EBIT coverage is **5.9x**. And the guide explicitly separates **$90–95B of gross capex from no more than $70B of net cash capex** — a $20–25B gap carried by leases and partner financing. The bear has been quoting the gross number and computing burn from it, which overstates the cash call by roughly a quarter.

### Bear — second exchange
The lumpiness point is fair and I'll accept the correction: one quarter does not make a run rate, and I would not have annualised $85B either. Take it as what it is — **the first deceleration in the only metric that matters, unresolved until December.** But notice what accepting lumpiness costs the bull: if RPO is lumpy and unforecastable, then the "7.4 years of backlog, half converting in 36 months" framing is not the reliable annuity his opening implied. He cannot have it be a dependable conversion schedule *and* too lumpy to read.

On net capex I accept the correction outright — $70B net, not $92.5B, and my burn figure overstated. On diversification: "the non-OpenAI backlog more than doubled" is management's phrasing, and doubling a minority still leaves roughly half the book with one loss-making counterparty. I am not relying on my own opinion here. **S&P — which has access and a fiduciary reputation — named OpenAI as a key credit risk while cutting the company to one notch above junk.** That is a third party with no axe, and this desk has spent two weeks learning to prefer exactly that kind of evidence.

Last thing, and it is the one the bull never addressed. **A year ago this same news moved the stock +36% in a day to $345.72. This year it opened up and closed the gap within the session, on a stock already down 54%.** He calls the de-rating "already priced." ### Research Manager ruling

**Recommendation**: **Hold**

**Rationale**: This was the best-argued debate the desk has run, and both researchers conceded material points, which makes the ruling cleaner than usual.

**The bull won two exchanges outright.** The lumpiness correction was right and the bear accepted it: a single quarter of +$26B after +$85B is not a run rate, and annualising it would be exactly the error the bull identified. The net-capex correction was also right and also accepted — **$70B net cash capex, not $92.5B gross** — and the bear's burn arithmetic did overstate by roughly a quarter. Neither point is small.

**The bear won the ruling anyway, on three grounds the bull did not dislodge.**

First, the bull's concession on lumpiness cost him his own framing, and the bear caught it: **RPO cannot be both a dependable 36-month conversion annuity and too lumpy to read one quarter of.** The opening thesis leaned on the annuity version; the defence required the lumpy version.

Second, the net-capex correction narrows the hole without closing it. **$70B of net cash capex against $90B of revenue is 78% of revenue**, free cash flow remains negative, **the CFO gave no timetable**, and the $20–25B credited back is lease and partner financing — an obligation relocated rather than removed.

Third, and decisively, **both metrics this desk names in advance came back negative**. The operating metric — sequential RPO additions — fell 69%. The balance-sheet metric — **BBB−, one notch above junk, 500% debt-to-equity, ~$131B net debt, negative FCF with no path stated, and an equity raise undertaken because more debt would trigger a downgrade** — is the weakest the desk has assessed. **That is the BKE configuration**, and on 2026-09-10 it produced a Hold on the cheapest stock the desk had priced. Consistency requires the same answer here.

**Why not Underweight.** Nothing is deteriorating operationally: revenue +30%, OCI +121%, both lines beat, GPU utilisation 97.9%, guidance raised to $90B and 34% constant-currency growth. Underweight means reduce, and there is no operational case for reducing a business growing at that rate. The risk here is financial and structural — real, unfunded, and unhandicappable — but it is a risk, not a realised impairment.

**Why not Overweight, given ~20x for 34% growth.** Because the multiple is the compensation for exactly these risks, and this desk's job is to decide whether it is *sufficient*. With roughly half a $664B backlog resting on one loss-making private counterparty that S&P named a key credit risk, with bookings decelerating 69% in the quarter capex peaked, and with the sell side spreading targets **3.6x from $110 to $400**, the honest answer is that nobody — including this desk — can size that compensation. The market's own reaction settles it: **a year ago this news was worth +36% in a day; this year the gap closed within the session.**

**Strategic Actions**: No position. **No stop is to be proposed** — the price cannot be pinned tighter than $157–165 on an 11.3% range day, and a stop set against a band is a stop against nothing. Revisit on two events: **Q2 FY27 results in December**, where the single question is whether sequential RPO additions re-accelerate off $26B, and **the equity offering**, which converts a stated intention into a known dilution.
