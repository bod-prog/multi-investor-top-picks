**Growth decomposition**: dealers +5% × QARSD +7% ≈ **revenue +13%**. Clean and
self-reconciling — the same shape as the EXPO run's headcount × utilization ×
rate.

QARSD growth is attributed to four drivers: **add-on product adoption,
like-for-like price increases, subscription upgrades, and improvements in lead
quantity and quality** — with new product adoption the largest contributor to
sequential gains for the **third consecutive quarter**.

**A data-integrity problem in the decisive metric, and how it is handled.**
The source reports consolidated QARSD of **$8,134**, with US at **$2,568** and
international at **$5,566**. Those two figures sum to exactly $8,134 — but a
*consolidated* QARSD must be a weighted average, which would fall **between**
$2,568 and $5,566, not equal their sum. Either the source constructed the
consolidated figure by addition, or the segment labels are wrong. Compounding
the doubt, international QARSD at more than double the US level and growing
faster (+11.2% vs +8.0%) is counterintuitive for a US-centric marketplace.

**The absolute QARSD dollar levels are therefore not used anywhere in this
report.** The *growth rates* are, because they arrive in a separate sentence
and reconcile independently: 5% dealer growth and 7% QARSD growth combining to
13% revenue growth is internally consistent and matches the reported revenue.
That split is defensible, but it must be stated rather than assumed — this desk
has spent the week catching exactly this kind of source failure.

**The margin fact the bull could not explain away**: adjusted EBITDA grew
**7%** while revenue grew **13%**. At $84.7M on $251.0M that is a 33.7% EBITDA
margin — excellent in absolute terms, but roughly 190bp below the prior year's
implied level.

Comparison against this desk's two Holds from earlier today:
