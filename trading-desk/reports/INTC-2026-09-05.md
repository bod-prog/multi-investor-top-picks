## Risk review

### Aggressive
Holding at $95.81 on a company that just printed its fastest revenue growth
since 2011 is the same reasoning that kept people out of the entire AI cycle.
The 14A commitment window is guided for H2 2026 into H1 2027 — it can land
inside four months, and it is the highest-convexity catalyst in large-cap
semis. A Hold captures exactly none of it. Consensus target is +20% and the
high target is $200, more than double.

And look at what the proposal is actually waiting for: the 200-day EMA at
$79.18, a 17% drawdown from here. The stock is up 83.6% YTD *because* it never
handed anyone that entry. Setting a re-engagement level below the market and
calling it discipline is how a correct thesis produces zero P&L.

### Conservative
The stop is the flaw. $86.66 is 9.6% below spot, which sounds prudent until you
price the realized volatility: this stock fell from $140.94 to $88.97 in ten
weeks — a 37% drawdown — and moves 4–5% on days with no company news at all.
A 9.6% stop on that instrument is inside the noise band. It gets taken out on a
sector rotation and the position is either re-entered higher or abandoned at
the low.

Second, the entire bull case reduces to one unsigned customer commitment. If
14A slips again — and the base rate for Intel foundry timelines slipping is
close to certainty — there is no valuation floor between 42.8x and the sector
median near 22x. That gap is roughly a 48% haircut. Third, rising rate-hike
odds against a 42.8x multiple is the wrong direction on the wrong duration.
The Hold rating is right. The unaddressed risk sits in the *existing* position,
not the new one.

### Neutral
Both colleagues are arguing about a position the proposal does not take. The
trader called Hold with no new exposure; the aggressive convexity case and the
conservative stop critique are both litigating a trade that is not being
opened. The conservative point does land on legacy holdings, and should be
taken there — a 9.6% stop on a 37%-drawdown instrument is not protection.

What the proposal genuinely assumes, and could be wrong about, is two things.
First, it anchors on technicals from Sept 1–2 at $88.97 while the stock is
7.7% higher — the "below both EMAs" and "Strong Sell" readings may already be
false, and the desk is reasoning from a two-session-old picture it has not
refreshed. Second, it treats 42.8x versus a 22.28x sector median as a clean
92% premium, but Intel's earnings base is depressed and its capex is peaking
while peers are mid-cycle. The premium is real; it is mechanically overstated
by that comparison.

Neither error changes the rating. Both should stop the desk from reading Hold
as a bearish call. It is not one — it is the honest position when a verified
recovery and a verified premium cancel out.
