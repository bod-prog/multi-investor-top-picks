# Top picks — 2026-09-09
**Horizon**: 1m · **Style**: balanced · **Floor**: ~$2B market cap

Third run in three days. Thirteen names are now excluded by the decision log,
so the universe this skill searches is genuinely narrowing — and this report
delivers **four** names rather than the customary six, because that is what
survived verification. Two candidates were killed by the cross-check.

## Correcting this skill's own bias

The previous two reports both recorded the same criticism of themselves: the
catalyst angle did nearly all the work (4 of 6 names on 2026-09-07, 4 of 6
again on 2026-09-08), and the quantitative screen was skipped on the second run
because the first one returned little.

**This run led with the two under-used angles instead**, and both produced:

- **Insider buying** — never used before. Produced TSN, and with it the most
  interesting single situation in this report.
- **A proper quantitative screen** — this time an ROIC-based one (ROIC >25%,
  market cap $2–10B, Piotroski F-Score ≥6, Health Score ≥50) rather than a
  low-P/E list. Produced CARG, QLYS, EXPO and BKE — mid-caps, not the mega-caps
  the theme angle kept returning.

The correction worked. The result is also the most sector-diversified shortlist
of the three, and that was an outcome rather than a design choice.

### 1. QLYS — Qualys · $171.00 (2026-09-09) · $5.92B

The best-verified name in three runs: the cross-check landed at $171.10 against
a quoted $171.00, on data from today's session ($167.50–$171.63). Surfaced by
the ROIC screen at **32%**, inside a filter also requiring a Piotroski F-Score
of at least 6 and low leverage.

Qualys sells cloud-based vulnerability management and compliance — recurring
subscription revenue in a security budget line that is among the last things an
enterprise cuts. The stock has drifted from roughly $180.68 in late August to
$171.00, about 5% in two weeks, with no catalyst found in this search.

What breaks it: it is software into a live rate hike, which is the same
headwind this desk has now applied to IOT and ESTC — and unlike either of them,
**no earnings catalyst was found here**. This name comes from a screen, not an
event, which means the evidence for it is structural rather than timely.

### 2. EXPO — Exponent · $68.35 (2026-09-05) · $3.25B

Named in the screen's "highest-conviction intersection of ROIC, quality and
growth in the $2–10B universe." Exponent is an engineering and scientific
consultancy — failure analysis, product-defect investigation, litigation
support. Asset-light, credential-driven, and structurally counter-cyclical in
part: when products fail and disputes multiply, its work increases.

What breaks it: a headline in this search flagged **"top executive sells
thousands of Exponent shares."** No amount or date could be sourced, so it is
reported as a flag rather than a quantified signal — but it points the opposite
way from the quality screen that surfaced the name, and it is exactly the kind
of counter-evidence a screen cannot see. Verify insider flow before acting.

### 3. CARG — CarGurus · $36.56 (2026-08-28) · $3.22B

The highest ROIC in the entire screen at **79.5%** — an online marketplace with
minimal capital intensity, where incremental listings cost almost nothing to
serve. Cross-check within 1.1%.

What breaks it: two things. The data is **12 days old**, the stalest on this
list, so the price may have moved materially. And the business is a used-car
marketplace, so its volumes ride on auto lending — which puts it on the wrong
side of the same rate call that dominated yesterday's TRU analysis. A very high
ROIC on a rate-sensitive volume business is a quality metric attached to a
cyclical exposure.

### 4. TSN — Tyson Foods · $51.54 (2026-09-07) · cap not verifiable

The most interesting situation here and deliberately ranked last, because the
evidence position is the weakest.

**The signal**: Jeffrey K. Schomburger, **President and CEO Elect** and a
director, bought **19,450 shares at a weighted average of $51.497 on
2026-09-04 — about $1,001,616**. The stock trades at $51.54, effectively at his
purchase price and just above its 52-week low of $50.56. An incoming chief
executive committing a million dollars of his own money at the low, days before
taking the job, is among the highest-information insider signals available.

**The problem**: Tyson has **cut its 2026 outlook on severe cattle shortages
and rising costs.** The buy and the guidance cut point in opposite directions,
and both are recent.

That tension is the finding, and it is why this name is on the list rather than
resolved by it. Either the incoming CEO sees past a cyclical input-cost problem
that the market is pricing as structural, or he is signalling confidence with a
purchase that is small relative to his incoming compensation. Nothing in this
search distinguishes them — but consumer staples is one of the sectors the
verified 2026 rotation favours, which makes the question worth answering rather
than dismissing.

What breaks it: cattle-cycle input costs are not a quarter-long problem, and
"CEO bought at the low" is a headline that reads identically whether the low is
a bottom or a waypoint.

## The search's own bias

- **The angle correction worked**, and it is the main methodological result of
  this run: leading with insider buying and an ROIC screen produced four names
  from two angles that had previously produced almost nothing, and eliminated
  the catalyst-angle dominance both prior reports criticised in themselves.
- **But data quality was materially worse today.** One outright failed
  cross-check, one undated price, one inconclusive cross-check, and one name
  whose data is 12 days old. Only QLYS and EXPO carry clean, dated
  verification. Three of four names on this list would not have passed the
  standard applied to the other one.
- **No catalyst-driven name survived.** Every name here comes from a screen or
  an insider filing, which means none has a dated event pushing it. That is the
  mirror image of the previous two runs and it changes what the list is: these
  are structural candidates, not timely ones.
- **A sector figure conflicts with earlier runs.** This search reported energy
  up **11.2% year-to-date** as the best-performing sector; the 2026-09-07 run
  reported energy up **22% YTD**. Both cannot be right. The discrepancy is
  unresolved and neither figure is used in this report's reasoning.
- **The universe is narrowing.** Thirteen exclusions, plus a growing list of
  rejected dead ends. Future runs will need either wider constraints, a
  different market, or the watchlist to be provided so exclusions can be
  precise rather than defensive.
