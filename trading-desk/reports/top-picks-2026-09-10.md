### 1. HONA — Honeywell Aerospace · $156.40 (2026-09-08) · ~$51B

**The only name here confirmed by two independent angles**, which is why it
ranks first.

Honeywell completed the aerospace spinoff on **2026-06-29**, with HONA
beginning trading as a standalone aerospace supplier while Honeywell
Technologies kept the HON ticker after a 1-for-2 reverse split. Freshly spun
entities are the classic special-situations setup: index funds and mandates
that cannot hold the new entity sell it indiscriminately in the first months,
which is where the mispricing window sits. HONA is roughly ten weeks into that
window.

Then the second angle landed on the same name independently: **an executive at
the newly spun-off aerospace company bought 6,400 shares on 2026-09-08.** Per
the rule this desk settled yesterday, an insider signal is evidence when it
carries a name, date, amount and price — this one has a date, a share count and
a role, but **no dollar amount or price was sourced**, so it is reported as a
partial signal rather than a full one.

What breaks it: nothing is known here about the business itself. No revenue, no
margin, no guidance, no debt load — and a spinoff's capital structure is
frequently the whole story, because parents routinely load debt onto the entity
being separated. **This name is a setup, not a thesis**, and the `trading-desk`
run would need to start with the balance sheet.

### 2. VICI — VICI Properties · $25.27 (2026-09-09) · $27.82B

The cleanest cross-check of the three, to two cents. And the situation is a
genuine divergence: the stock opened at **$25.27 against a 52-week low of
$25.34** — meaning it is trading **below its own 52-week low** — while a
headline the same week reads *"VICI Properties stock holds near its yearly low
**as guidance rises**."* Price falling while guidance rises is exactly the
disagreement the contrarian angle exists to find.

VICI is a gaming REIT: it owns casino real estate and collects rent under
long-term triple-net leases. The dividend is **$0.46 quarterly with an ex-date
of 2026-09-17**, against a stated yield of **7%**.

What breaks it, and it is not subtle: **a REIT is a bond substitute, and this
desk has verified across six runs that a September Fed hike is live** with
inflation at 4.2% and the median FOMC end-2026 dot at 3.8%. A 7% yield on a
falling REIT price is the market pricing rate risk, not a gift. The guidance
rising while the price falls is explained entirely if the market is
discounting the yield rather than the cash flow.
