### What this says about the desk's own macro call

### 1 · THG — The Hanover Insurance Group

**Why it is here**: the theme angle, and specifically the one sector the hike
mechanically helps. Insurers reinvest premium float into fixed income at higher
yields and — unlike banks — carry no deposit-flight risk, so a higher curve is
a durable earnings tailwind across reinvestment cycles rather than a one-off.
P&C industry net underwriting gain was **$31.7B in H1 2026, up nearly 3x year
over year**.

**What supports it, dated**: Q2 2026 net income **$191.6M, $5.38 per diluted
share**. Full-year 2025 delivered a record **operating ROE of 20.1%** (23.1% in
Q4) on **$6.3B of net written premiums**. A quarterly dividend of **$0.95** was
declared **2026-08-31**. A **$700M share repurchase** is planned — **8.6% of
the market cap** at today's price. The stock closed at an **all-time high of
$232.83 on 2026-09-15**, +16.61% over 90 days and **+30.03% year to date**.
Management holds a **virtual strategic outlook event today, 2026-09-17**.

**Verification**: shares outstanding **35,149,316**; market cap **$7.76B**.
Cross-check **$7.76B ÷ 35,149,316 = $220.77** against a $222.89 quote dated
2026-08-17 — a **0.95%** match. Note precisely what that validates: the cap and
that quote are from the same date, so the check confirms the **share count**,
not today's price. The 09-15 ATH close of $232.83 is separately sourced, and
implies a current cap of **~$8.18B** (derived, not fetched). A third check
reconciles independently: **$191.6M ÷ $5.38 = 35.61M diluted shares** against
35.15M basic — the dilution gap is the right size and the right direction.
Dividend yield at $232.83 is **1.63%**.

**The single thing most likely to break it**: this is a **momentum entry, not a
value one, and the report will not pretend otherwise**. The stock is at an
all-time high after a 30% year. Everything attractive here — record ROE, the
buyback, the rate tailwind — is visible to everyone and has already been paid
for. The event today is a *known* date, which means the move may happen before
any reader acts on this. Secondary flag: one source gives Q2 EPS as **$5.31**
and another **$5.38**, most likely operating versus GAAP diluted. Unresolved.

reported here at that weight and no heavier. But it points at the real risk: if
channel inventory was obscured, the disclosures a thesis would rest on are the
thing in question, and this desk's binding constraint is already verification.

## Findings

**The non-US widening has a cost the proposal did not price.** The 09-10 run
logged "widen the market" as the next step because five runs had exhausted the
US angles. This run tried it, and the result is worth recording: **widening the
market widens the verification burden proportionally**, and verification has
been the binding constraint for three consecutive runs. A US name needs one
search to pin price and cap; a Tokyo or London name needs several, plus a
currency, plus an exchange convention. The angle is not wrong — the valuation
gap is real and sourced — but it should be attempted as a **dedicated run**
with the whole search budget behind it, not as one angle among four.

**Search bias**: three of four angles converged on **rate-sensitive
financials**, which is the market telling the search what it just did rather
than four independent ideas. The healthcare name is the only one that did not
come from the rate theme, and it arrived through the contrarian angle, not the
screen. A two-name list from four angles is a low yield, and it is honest: the
sector cap would have rejected a third insurer even if one had verified.

**Sector coverage, for the first time.** Across fifteen prior names the desk
has never rated a **financial** or a **healthcare** company (COO was surfaced
and rejected on 09-11). Both names here are firsts, which is a point in the
angle-rotation discipline's favour — and also means neither benefits from any
accumulated method rules specific to those sectors.

**Verification remains the binding constraint — fourth consecutive run.** Of
roughly a dozen raw candidates, ten were rejected, **eight for want of a price,
a cap, or a date**.
