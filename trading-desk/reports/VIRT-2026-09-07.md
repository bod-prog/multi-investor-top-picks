**Investment Thesis**: This is a "right idea, wrong entry" Hold, and it is

$6.96 — with a Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin of 60.8% and a net income margin of
23.9%. At $64.82 that is roughly 9.3x adjusted trailing earnings (derived).
The macro backdrop is the one this business monetises: a live September Fed
decision, inflation at a three-year high, and the worst seasonal month of the
year.

The bear case never attacked any of that. It attacked the price and the
positioning, and it did so with the one category of evidence that is hard to
argue with — what management itself is doing. Insiders are net sellers by $23M
over twelve months, $12M in the last three, at an all-time high. Buybacks have
slowed while capital has been raised. Those are the revealed preferences of the
people with the best information about whether 9.3x on these earnings is cheap,
and they point the other way from the multiple.

The second decisive point is structural rather than sentimental: for a market
maker, a low multiple on record earnings is the signature of a cyclical peak,
not of value. Volatility is the input, volatility mean-reverts, and the "record
TTM" framing is therefore a warning label as much as an attraction. The
multiple is low *because* the market does not capitalise peak-cycle earnings at
full value.

What stops this from being an Underweight is that the catalyst is genuinely
near-dated. If the Fed hikes in September, the earnings do not revert — they
step up, and the entry price stops mattering. The desk is not rejecting the
idea; it is declining to pay 94% of the all-time high for a thesis that has an
identifiable trigger it can wait for.

### Conservative
The stop problem is not a technicality to be sized around — it is
disqualifying as the proposal stands, and I agree with the trader for once. A
26% gap to the nearest structural level means every intermediate stop is
arbitrary; you will be shaken out by noise or carried into a real decline. My
colleague's answer — "size it as a full loss" — is the correct construction and
should be stated as such rather than implied, because at 2% of book that is
2% written off on a coin flip.

Beyond that, three exposures the proposal does not price. The earnings are
peak-cycle by construction and a 9.3x multiple on peak earnings is not 9.3x.
Insiders sold $23M net over twelve months. And volume at 40% of average says
the ATH was made on participation that has since left — thin tape cuts both
ways, and it cuts harder on the way down.

One the desk has underweighted entirely: the reported exploration of a sale of
the agency brokerage unit. Execution Services is $2.2M of $11.6M daily ANTI —
the steadier fifth of the earnings. Selling it makes the remaining company a
purer, more volatile instrument than the one being analysed here. That is a
change to the risk profile of the asset itself, not to its price.

### Neutral
The aggressive case and the conservative case have, unusually, converged on the
same construction — a small position sized as a full loss — and are describing
it as a disagreement. That is worth naming, because the actual gap between them
is about 2% of a portfolio.

What the proposal genuinely assumes and could be wrong about, in order of
consequence:

First, that VIRT should be judged standalone at all. Its distinguishing feature
is that it pays when the rest of the book does not, and insurance is priced by
what it costs relative to what it protects — not by whether the insurer's own
shares look cheap. This desk analyses one ticker at a time by design, so it
structurally cannot see that. The honest disposition is to flag it for
`portfolio-review` rather than to settle it here, and a Hold from this desk
should not be read as an answer to the portfolio question.

Second, the technical foundations of the entry argument are undated. The 60-day
MA at $47.944, the 200-day at $39.87, the RSI at 50 — none carried an as-of
date. Every extension figure in this report, and the entire $48–52

Third, the bull's macro argument and the bear's peak-cycle argument are the
