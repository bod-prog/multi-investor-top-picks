### Neutral
Both officers are arguing well and both are overstating one thing.

The aggressive officer is right that sell-side ratings lag, and the desk should
concede it: a Hold after a 129% run is partly an artefact of the run. But the
argument does not survive contact with the *average target of $165 against a
$171 price*. A lagging rating explains a cautious recommendation; it does not
explain a fair-value estimate below the market. That distinction is what makes
this evidence rather than noise, and the ruling rested on the right half of it.

The conservative officer is right that margin optimisation and slow growth are
one fact — but "harvest" is a hypothesis, not a finding, and the report should
not let it harden. The margin expansion is equally consistent with operating
leverage in a subscription model at scale. Neither reading is available from
what was fetched, which is the actual state of knowledge.

Three things the desk should own:

First, **the streak question, in the other direction now.** The previous run
logged discomfort at three consecutive Overweights, each justified by a
different metric. This run breaks that streak — and the desk should be as
suspicious of a Hold that conveniently answers its own self-criticism as it was
of the third Overweight. The test is whether the reasoning would have produced

below spot is a fact independent of anything the desk did last week.

share on TRU. NRR on ESTC. NRR and billings here. Three runs, three times the
