### 1 · LSEG.L — London Stock Exchange Group

**Why it is here**: the catalyst angle, carried over as the one 09-17 lead with
a concrete, dated reason attached. Consensus EPS was upgraded **from 418p to
493p for 2026 (+17.9%)** and **from 444p to 538p for 2027 (+21.2%)**. LSEG is
not really an exchange any more — the Refinitiv acquisition made it a data and
analytics business with recurring subscription revenue, which is why upgrades
of that size are possible without a trading-volume boom behind them.

**Verification — the cleanest of this run**: price **8,220p as of 2026-09-17**,
previous close 8,238p. Market cap **£39.92bn**. Shares in issue reported as
**482.63M** by one source and **486,909,949** by another.

- **£39.92bn ÷ 482.63M = 8,271p** against the 8,238p previous close — **0.40%** ✓
- **£39.92bn ÷ 486.91M = 8,199p** — **0.48%** ✓

Both share counts pass, which is stronger than one passing: the small
discrepancy between them (basic versus issued) does not move the check.
Capitalisation is **~$52.3B** at GBP/USD 1.31 — far above the floor and deeply
liquid.

**Valuation**: at 8,220p against upgraded 2026 EPS of 493p, **16.7x forward**;
on 2027's 538p, **15.3x**.

**A third source reported a market cap of £49.73B and it was discarded**, not
reconciled: it is 25% above the other two readings, does not correspond to the
share count at any plausible price, and most likely mixes currencies. Recorded
rather than quietly dropped.

**The single thing most likely to break it**: the upgrades are the thesis and
they are also already public. A 17–21% consensus revision is not a secret —
16.7x forward for a data business is a *fair* multiple, not a cheap one, and
the desk has no evidence about what drove the revision. Nothing here identifies
whether the upgrade came from subscription growth, cost action, the buyback, or
an acquisition, and those have very different durability.

### 2 · 8035.T — Tokyo Electron

**Why it is here**: the theme angle. Japan's structural story is real and

statistics point at the index, and the mega-caps they name are the obvious
first-order holdings this skill exists to avoid. Tokyo Electron is the
semiconductor-equipment supplier underneath the AI capex cycle, which is a
second-order exposure to a theme the desk has already met from the other side

**What supports it, dated**: **H1 FY2027 forecast raised** to net sales

**Verification — passes on price, soft on the cross-check, and the report will
not smooth that over**: price **¥50,600 as of 2026-09-17** with a previous

- **¥24.13tn ÷ 454.30M = ¥53,115**, which is **4.97% above** the 09-17 price

That is a soft fail, and it sits in the band that rejected HCI at 4.0% last

## The currency problem, which is this run's real finding

A US-dollar investor buying a `.L` or `.T` listing takes two exposures, and the
desk can only analyse one of them.

**Spot, dated and sourced**: the Fed hiked to 4.00% on 2026-09-16 with markets
pricing **more than one further increase this year and roughly three more by
end-2027**. The **dollar index is up 1.4% on the week** and reached a
**seven-week high** on the hawkish decision. **GBP/USD ~1.31**; **USD/JPY
~160**, having moved from 159.23 to 160.03 through August.

**Forecast, and labelled as forecast rather than evidence**: consensus points
to a broadly stable near-term dollar followed by gradual weakness — GBP/USD
**1.38** and USD/JPY **160** by Q4 2026. If GBP/USD reached 1.38 that is a
**+5.3%** tailwind on a sterling holding; if the hawkish path extends instead,
it is a headwind of similar size.

So the currency is genuinely two-sided, and the desk will not pick the side
that flatters the list. What matters is that **a ±5% currency swing is the same
order of magnitude as the entire valuation gap** that justified looking abroad.

**And a specific problem with the handoff.** This skill's output goes into the
user's app for relative-strength scoring against a peer list. RS on a `.L` or
`.T` series is computed **in local currency**, so it measures a return the
dollar-based holder does not actually earn. Two names quoted in pence and yen,
ranked against a list quoted in dollars, are not being compared on the same
axis. That is not a reason to discard them — it is a reason to know what the
score means before acting on it, and it did not arise in any of the six prior
runs.

## Findings

**The non-US angle costs roughly double and adds a factor the desk cannot
price.** Two names from a full budget, against two names from *one of four
angles* in the 09-17 US run. The hazards are specific and new:

1. **Unit ambiguity** — pence quoted as GBP, a 100x error with no US analogue.
2. **Currency-inconsistent capitalisations** — Tokyo Electron's USD cap implies
   ¥149/USD against spot 160; LSEG had a £49.73B outlier 25% off the others.
   Both were caught only *because* the cross-check ran.
3. **Staleness is worse abroad** — the best available Clarkson price was three
   months old, where US names routinely resolve inside a week.
4. **The currency exposure itself**, which is ±5% and therefore comparable to
   the valuation gap that motivated the search.

**Recommendation: keep the angle, at roughly one run in four, and only when the
budget can be dedicated.** It should not return as one angle among four —
that configuration produced zero verified names on 09-17. It is not retired:
the valuation gap is real, and LSEG's file is the best-verified of the last two
runs.

**Search bias**: the Japan theme angle returned mega-caps almost exclusively.
That is a property of how the governance/buyback story is written up in English
— it is reported at index and champion-company level — and it means the theme
angle is structurally poor at producing non-US *mid*-caps. The catalyst angle
(which produced LSEG) travelled better.

**Sector coverage**: Financials and Technology, one each. Financials is a
second consecutive appearance after THG, which is worth watching if it recurs.

**Verification is the binding constraint — fifth consecutive run.** Of roughly a
dozen raw candidates, ten were rejected; **eight for want of a price, a
capitalisation, or a date.**
