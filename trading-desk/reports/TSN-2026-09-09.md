**Rating**: Overweight

**Executive Summary**: Half size around $52.09. Reserve the second half until

**Investment Thesis**: The decisive metric was named before searching — segment
operating margin by protein — and it reframed the entire situation on the first
attempt.

**The guidance cut is a beef story and nothing else.** Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended
27 June, reported ~10 August): beef posted a **$138M operating loss** on volume
down **15.9%** while pricing rose **12.1%**, and full-year beef guidance was
widened by $150M to a loss of **$(650)M–$(500)M**. Meanwhile **chicken
delivered $488M of adjusted segment operating income, up 8.9%, with margin
widening to 11.2% — and full-year chicken guidance of $1.90–2.05B was
REAFFIRMED.** Chicken alone more than covers beef's entire annual loss.

And the company-level result held: **adjusted EPS rose 9% to $0.99** in the very
quarter containing a $138M beef loss, on sales roughly flat at $13.9B.

**The insider signal is materially stronger than this morning's shortlist
knew.** Tyson cut fiscal 2026 guidance on **3 September** (adjusted operating
income to $1.85–2.05B). Jeffrey Schomburger bought **19,450 shares at a
weighted average $51.497 — about $1,001,616 — on 4 September**, *the day after
the cut*. He has sat on Tyson's board **since 2016**, so he has watched this
cattle cycle from inside for a decade, and he becomes CEO on **4 October**.
Buying into your own bad news, a month before you own the results, is close to
the most informed configuration insider buying comes in.

**But the rating does not rest on it.** Strip the purchase out entirely and what
remains is: chicken reaffirmed and covering beef, adjusted EPS growing 9%,
roughly 13x annualised earnings, a **3.92% dividend yield**, a price 25% below
the 52-week high, and named targets 25–44% above spot. The insider buy is
corroboration, not foundation — and the report says so because otherwise the
whole thesis would rest on one person's million dollars.

What keeps this below Buy is the bear's mechanism, which is real: **cattle herd
rebuilding tightens slaughter supply before it eases it**, because ranchers
retain heifers to rebuild. The trough may be ahead rather than behind. The
technical picture agrees — the stock has broken every sourced level and made a
new 52-week low this month.

**Price Target**: not set. **Both named actions are cuts, from high levels**:
BofA to **$65** from $68, **Neutral** (+24.8% from spot); Barclays (Benjamin
Theurer) to **$75** from $76, **Overweight** (+44.0%), though the Barclays
action may predate Q3. Consensus across 13 analysts (S&P Global) is **Hold** at
an average of **$68.75** (+32.0%).

The shape is unusual and worth naming: **the revisions point down while the
levels sit far above the price.** The stock has fallen much further than the
sell side has cut, which means either the targets are anchored and lagging or
the market has overshot.

**Time Horizon**: 9–18 months — longer than this desk's other calls, because a
cattle cycle is measured in years, not quarters.
