# GME — GameStop Corp.
**Analysis date**: 2026-09-11 · **Depth**: standard

## Decision
**Rating**: Overweight

**Executive Summary**: Half position now near $21.26, half held until Ryan Cohen resolves the eBay question — an **event trigger, not a price trigger**, following the TSN precedent of 2026-09-09. Stop $19.50, just under the 50-day MA at $19.70 and Cohen's own $20.3759 purchase price, for ~8.3% risk; the deeper support cluster is $17.79–18.04. Maximum 2–3% of book: this is a transformation trade with a large unhedged equity position attached, not a core holding. Horizon is governed by two events rather than months — the eBay decision, live now, and Q3 results for whether collectibles growth holds.

**Investment Thesis**: This is the first run in this desk's history where **the decisive operating metric came back strongly positive**, and it did so with three independent reconciliations rather than one. GameStop's Q2 gross margin went from 29.1% to **43.7%** — 1,460 basis points — because collectibles became the company. Collectibles revenue rose **57% to $356.3M and is now 45.1% of sales**, up from 23.4%, while video games fell to 33.3% from 51%. Both stated figures check exactly against the totals ($356.3M ÷ $790.2M = 45.09%; implied prior-year collectibles of $227.5M gives +56.6%), and the segment mix sums to 100.0%.

The bear's most natural attack — that "record operating income" on a 19% sales decline must be an accounting artifact — does not survive arithmetic. Derived from the sourced figures: gross profit rose **+22.1%** ($282.9M → $345.3M) while implied SG&A fell **−14.5%** ($216.5M → $185.1M). Operating income of $160.2M is fully explained by those two lines with no residual, which means no disposal gain or interest income is needed to account for it. Gross profit grew 22% while revenue fell 19%. That is a genuine mix transformation with store-closure operating leverage on top, not a one-off.

The balance sheet then does something unusual: it makes the equity cheap in a way that is checkable. Verified at 2026-08-01 — cash $4,854.3M, marketable securities $206.0M, digital assets $294.1M (stated total $5,354.4M, and the three sum to exactly that), plus 43.4M eBay shares at ~$4,947M. Pro-forma for the convertible exchange that closed around 2026-09-03, net liquid assets are **$7.14B, or $14.16 per share against a $21.26 price**. The market is paying roughly **$7.10/share — $3.58B — for the operating business**, which against a full-year adjusted EBITDA guide of more than $650M is between 5.5x and 8.1x depending on how much interest income that guide contains.

What holds this at Overweight rather than Buy is not the business. It is that **$4.947B — 46% of the entire market capitalisation — sits in a single unhedged position in one stock**, acquired for $4.4B of cash in a hostile approach that eBay's board rejected in May as "neither credible nor attractive," explicitly citing GameStop's governance. That outcome is being decided right now by one person, and today's commentary describes him weighing three options. This desk held VICI three days ago partly because of "a refinancing test nobody here can handicap." The same honesty applies here, and it caps the size rather than the rating, because unlike VICI the operating business underneath is visibly improving rather than merely stable.

**Price Target**: Not set, and deliberately. The only analyst material that could be sourced is **stale 2025 data** (Wedbush Underperform, targets of $10 and $13.50 dated June 2025 and earlier). Setting a target from that would be inventing one. What can be stated instead is an asset-backed reference rather than a forecast: **net liquid assets of $14.16/share**, verified and marked, is 67% of the current price.

**Time Horizon**: Event-governed, not calendar-governed. The eBay decision is live. Q3 results (late November / December) test whether collectibles growth holds through the category's seasonal peak — which is also its most cyclical moment.

### Fundamentals
**GameStop is no longer usefully described as a video game retailer, and analysing it as one produces the wrong answer.**

Start with the operating business, because it is the part that has actually changed. Q2 net sales fell 18.7% to $790.2M — and gross margin rose from 29.1% to **43.7%**. Those two facts are the same fact. Collectibles — trading cards, apparel, toys, pop-culture merchandise — grew **57% to $356.3M** and are now **45.1% of revenue**, against 23.4% a year ago. Video games collapsed to 33.3% of sales from 51%, which on the implied dollars is about **−47%**. Pre-owned fell about **−32%**. The company is not shrinking uniformly; it is swapping a low-margin category for a high-margin one, and the swap is roughly half complete.

The operating leverage is real and can be demonstrated rather than asserted. Deriving from the sourced figures: **gross profit rose 22.1%** to $345.3M while **implied SG&A fell 14.5%** to $185.1M, and those two lines account for the entire $160.2M of operating income with nothing left over. Store closures — more than 470 confirmed for fiscal 2025, and 727 US stores closed in the twelve months to March 2026 — are doing exactly what they are supposed to do.

Now the part that dominates the valuation. As of 2026-08-01 the company held **$5,354.4M** in cash, securities and digital assets, and **43.4M eBay shares worth ~$4,947M**. Against **$2.8B** of remaining 0.00% convertible notes (post-exchange), net liquid assets are **$7.14B, or $14.16 per share** — **67% of the $21.26 price**. The residual claim on the operating business is **$7.10/share, or $3.58B**, against adjusted EBITDA guided above $650M.

**A correction to a widely circulated claim.** The "sum-of-the-parts bargain — cash and Bitcoin close to GME's price" framing rests on the **$9B of cash reported at 2026-01-31**. That cash no longer exists in that form: **$4.4B of it was spent buying eBay shares.** Anyone adding "$9B cash plus a $4.9B eBay stake" is counting the same $4.4B twice. The verified 2026-08-01 cash figure is $4,854.3M, and the decline is explicitly attributed to converting the eBay derivative into a direct equity position. The company is cheaper than a retailer, but it is not free.

Capital allocation is the open question and it cuts both ways. Management has: bought back stock implicitly through the convert structure, issued $4.2B of zero-coupon converts, spent $4.4B on a single equity stake, bought 4,710 Bitcoin and then pledged 4,709 of them as collateral in a covered-call program (booking a **$75M loss on digital assets** this quarter), and exchanged $1.4B of notes for 55.5M shares plus $358.4M cash. That is a great deal of financial engineering for a company whose operating turnaround is the actually impressive thing.

### News & macro
**The eBay situation is the dominant fact and it is unresolved as of today.**

The sequence: GameStop bought 5% of eBay on 2026-02-04; proposed to acquire the company at **$125.00/share, ~$56B**, in early May; **eBay's board rejected it on 2026-05-12** as "neither credible nor attractive," citing financing uncertainty, operational risk, and **GameStop's governance**. GameStop kept buying — 6.6% on 05-20, 7.8% on 05-28, and now **43.4M shares, close to 10%**. On **2026-08-10** Bloomberg reported Cohen **weighing pulling the offer**. By September the reporting describes a possible pivot to a **partnership or joint venture** in which eBay would use GameStop's ~1,600 US stores as physical infrastructure for trading cards and collectibles. **Today, 2026-09-11**, commentary describes him weighing three options.

That last point deserves emphasis rather than a footnote: a position worth **46% of the company's market capitalisation** has an outcome that is genuinely undetermined *as this report is written*, and the range of outcomes is wide — withdraw and hold or sell the stake, negotiate a commercial partnership, or escalate.

The commercial logic of the partnership version is the most interesting thing in this file. GameStop's transformation is *into collectibles*; eBay's highest-margin growth categories *are* trading cards and collectibles; GameStop has 1,600 physical locations for authentication, grading, trade-in and fulfilment. Those are complementary in a way the takeover was not, which is presumably why it is being considered.

Also this quarter: the **sale of the French business** (one of three cited reasons for the sales decline) and the **absence of the Nintendo Switch 2 launch comparison** — the latter being a pure calendar effect that will lap.

**Macro**, verified this morning: August CPI **+0.4% m/m, 3.4% y/y**, with **core easing to 2.4%** and gasoline more than a third of the monthly increase; traders price a **69% chance of a hike** at the FOMC on **2026-09-16, 2:00 PM ET**; Fed funds have been **3.50–3.75% all year**. Two channels matter here and they point opposite ways. Higher-for-longer rates *help* — GameStop earns interest on roughly $5B of cash and pays 0.00% on its converts, which is close to a perfect rate position. But collectibles are discretionary consumer spending, and a consumer squeezed by $106 Brent is the marginal buyer of trading cards. The equity has an unusual shape: a rate beneficiary on the balance sheet wrapped around a discretionary retailer.

## Researcher debate

### Bull — opening
Two numbers, and I want them taken together because separately each is misleading.

**Gross margin 29.1% to 43.7%.** Fourteen hundred and sixty basis points in a year. That does not happen from cost-cutting; it happens because the company sells something different than it used to. Collectibles grew **57% to $356.3M** and are now **45.1% of revenue** against 23.4%. Video games, the old business, fell to a third of sales. GameStop is half-converted into a collectibles retailer and the market is still pricing the half it is leaving.

And before the bear reaches for "record operating income on falling sales must be an accounting trick" — I have done the arithmetic. Gross profit **rose 22.1%**, from $282.9M to $345.3M, while implied SG&A **fell 14.5%**, from $216.5M to $185.1M. Those two lines account for the entire $160.2M of operating income with no residual. No disposal gain is needed to explain it, no interest income is needed to explain it. **Revenue fell 19% and gross profit rose 22%.** That is what a mix shift looks like when it works.

**Second number: $14.16.** That is net liquid assets per share — cash, securities, digital assets and the eBay stake, less the remaining $2.8B of converts, pro-forma for the exchange that closed on 3 September. The stock is $21.26. **Sixty-seven percent of the price is marked, liquid assets**, and the market is paying $7.10 a share — $3.58B — for a business guiding to more than $650M of adjusted EBITDA. Call it five to eight times, depending on how much of that guide is interest income.

Third, and the reason to act rather than watch: **Ryan Cohen bought 1,000,000 shares for $20,375,900 at $20.3759 on 10 September.** By this desk's own rule — a signal is evidence when it carries a name, a date, an amount and a price — that clears all four, at twenty times the dollar size of the TSN purchase that carried a rating three days ago. He bought *after* 55.5 million shares of dilution landed, and while holding the decision on a $4.9B position. There is no better-informed buyer available.

### Bear — reply
The bull's operating arithmetic is right and I am not going to waste a turn disputing it. I will dispute what it is worth, and then I will tell you what he did not price.

**On the transformation.** Total sales fell **18.7%**. Video games — still a third of this company — fell about **47%**, and pre-owned about **32%**. Collectibles is growing 57% and it is *still not enough* to hold the top line flat. The bull describes a company half-converted; I describe a race between a category growing 57% off a small base and two categories collapsing off a large one, and right now **the collapse is winning**. He is asking me to extrapolate the numerator and ignore the denominator.

**And on what collectibles is.** Trading cards is the most reliably cyclical category in retail. The 2020–21 Pokémon and sports-card boom went bust so hard it took grading companies and distributors with it. A 57% growth rate in trading cards is exactly what the top of that cycle looks like from inside it. The bull has one year of data and is pricing it as a trend.

**Now the thing he skipped.** **$4.947 billion — 46% of this company's entire market capitalisation — is in one stock.** Not an index, not a hedge: 43.4 million shares of eBay, bought with **$4.4 billion of cash**, in a hostile approach that eBay's board rejected in May as *"neither credible nor attractive,"* citing financing uncertainty, operational risk, and — read this one twice — **GameStop's governance**. A third party with a fiduciary duty looked at this management team and named governance as a reason to refuse.

The bull's $14.16 of "net liquid assets" is doing a lot of work in his thesis, and **$4.947B of that $7.14B is not liquid in any sense that matters** — it is a near-10% activist block in a company that has rejected you. You cannot sell it quickly without moving the price, and its value swings with eBay's stock, not GameStop's business. Strip it out and net liquid assets are $2.2B, about $4.35 a share.

**And the $238M gain the bull is quietly enjoying?** Mark-to-market. It is the single largest component of a GAAP EPS of $0.51 against an adjusted $0.27. It reverses as easily as it arrived.

### Bull — second exchange
The bear's strongest point is the eBay concentration and I am going to concede its shape before I answer it: yes, $4.947B in one position is concentration this desk would criticise in any portfolio, and yes, an activist block is less liquid than cash. I will not pretend a near-10% stake sells at the screen price.

But he has made an error of direction. He says strip the eBay stake out and net liquid assets are $4.35 a share. **Strip it out and you must also strip out what was paid for it** — $4.4B of cash that is no longer on the balance sheet *because it became those shares*. You cannot remove the asset and keep the hole. The relevant question is not whether the stake is liquid; it is whether $4.4B of cash was converted into something worth more or less than $4.4B. As of 1 August it was marked at **$4,947M — about $550M more than cost.** The trade is up. He is arguing it might reverse, which is true of every marked asset, and is not the same as it being worth nothing.

**On the cycle argument.** He says one year of collectibles growth is a cycle, not a trend. Fair challenge — so test it against the thing that is not cyclical: **1,600 physical stores**. The reason the eBay conversation has moved from a rejected takeover to a possible partnership is that eBay's highest-margin growth categories *are* trading cards and collectibles, and it has no physical footprint for authentication, grading, trade-in or fulfilment. GameStop has 1,600 of them. If a trading-card cycle is running, the company with the physical infrastructure captures the durable part of it. That is not a boom-chasing inventory bet; it is the one structural asset this company has that Amazon cannot replicate.

**On governance**, one correction. eBay's board cited governance *while refusing a hostile bid*. That is what boards say about hostile bidders. It is not an audit finding, and treating a defensive press statement as independent evidence is exactly the kind of sourcing this desk has thrown out six times in two weeks.

### Bear — second exchange
The cash-hole point is well made and I accept it: the stake is marked $550M above a $4.4B cost, and removing the asset while keeping the cash was sloppy. Take my net-liquid figure as wrong and the bull's $14.16 as the fair one, with the caveat that **$4.9B of it prices off eBay's chart, not GameStop's business.** That caveat is not rhetorical — it means two-thirds of the "cheapness" in this thesis is a bet on a stock nobody in this debate has analysed.

On governance I will meet him halfway and then take something back. He is right that a target board disparaging a hostile bidder is self-interested and weak evidence. But I am not relying on eBay's opinion. I am relying on **what this management team actually did**, which is a matter of record: issued $4.2B of converts, put $4.4B — nearly half the company's cash — into a single stock in a takeover the target refused, bought 4,710 Bitcoin and pledged 4,709 of them as collateral in a **covered-call options program**, booked a **$75M loss on digital assets** this quarter, and then exchanged $1.4B of notes for 55.5M shares plus $358.4M of cash. **That is the record.** eBay's board did not invent it; it read it.

And here is what the bull's insider argument omits. He calls Cohen's purchase the best-informed buy available, at twenty times TSN's size. Size it properly. Cohen holds about **43.08M shares, roughly $916M at today's price. His $20.4M purchase is 2.2% of his own position.** Schomburger at Tyson bought $1.0M as an incoming CEO — plausibly a serious fraction of his liquid wealth. Cohen added two percent to a stake he already had. It is a real signal and it is a *cheap* one, and this desk's rule counts the four attributes without ever asking what the purchase cost the buyer.

Finally, the thing neither of us can fix: **the decision is live right now.** Today's reporting has him weighing three options on a position worth 46% of the market cap. We are not analysing a company; we are analysing a company plus a pending decision by one man, and the debate cannot handicap it.

### Research Manager ruling

**Recommendation**: **Overweight**

**Rationale**: The bear won more exchanges than the bull and still loses the ruling, which needs explaining.

The bull's core claim survived every attack and was never seriously contested: **gross profit rose 22.1% while revenue fell 18.7%**, with implied SG&A down 14.5%, and those two lines fully account for a $160.2M operating income with no residual. The bear explicitly declined to dispute the arithmetic. This is the **first time in this desk's history that the Phase-1 decisive operating metric has come back strongly positive** — HONA's was negative, VICI's mixed, BKE's negative on both counts — and it arrived with three independent reconciliations (the 45.09% share check, the +56.6% growth check, and the mix summing to 100.0%).

The bear won three genuine points and they shape the *size*, not the direction:

1. **The denominator problem is real.** Collectibles at +57% is not yet outrunning video games at −47% off a larger base. The top line is still falling and the transformation is roughly half done.
2. **The eBay stake is 46% of market capitalisation in one unhedged position**, and the bull conceded it does not sell at the screen price. The bear's correction — that two-thirds of the balance-sheet "cheapness" prices off eBay's chart rather than GameStop's business — stands and is the most important sentence in the debate.
3. **The insider-signal sizing point is the sharpest thing either researcher said.** Cohen's $20.4M is **2.2% of his own $916M position**, against a TSN purchase that was plausibly a large fraction of the buyer's liquid wealth. The desk's insider rule counts name, date, amount and price, and never asks what the purchase *cost the buyer*. That is a defect in the rule and it is logged as such. The signal is downgraded from "strongest the funnel has produced" to "real but cheap."

The bull also won two exchanges outright. The cash-hole correction was decisive — the bear withdrew a figure after being shown he had removed the eBay asset while keeping the cash that bought it. And the governance point was correctly discounted: a target board's characterisation of a hostile bidder is a defensive statement, not evidence, which is the same standard this desk used to throw out six sentiment aggregates. The bear then recovered well by grounding governance in the *record of actions* rather than eBay's opinion, and that version stands.

**Why Overweight rather than Hold.** Compare this directly to the three Holds of 2026-09-10. HONA was held because its price could not be established; VICI because no stop could be placed; BKE because it was cheaper but slower with both decisive metrics negative. GME has a verified price with three independent anchors, a dense support cluster giving a placeable stop, and **both decisive metrics positive**.
