## Risk review

### Aggressive
Rule of 40 is a screening heuristic and this desk is using it as a verdict. It
was designed to rank private SaaS companies for growth-equity investors; it says
nothing about what a share of a profitable, cash-generative business at 26.8x
forward earnings is worth.

Look at what actually happened after the print rather than at the score. Piper
Sandler raised its target from **$85 to $130** — a 53% revision. Nobody moves a
target by half after a rounding-adjustment quarter; that is an analyst rebuilding
a model. Jefferies called it a clean beat with constant-currency acceleration
and went to $120. Canaccord went $80 to $120. Three separate houses independently
concluded the same thing.

And consider the desk's own inconsistency in the other direction. Yesterday it
bought IOT at 10.7x sales and conceded in writing that the size of the premium
was unexplained. Today it is hesitating at 4.7x. If an unexplained 2.8x premium
was worth half a position, a 1.2x premium with a 21% EPS beat behind it deserves
more than the same half.

### Conservative
This will be the desk's **third consecutive Overweight**, and each has been
justified by a different metric: Rule of 40 for IOT, forward P/E for TRU,
relative cheapness for ESTC. When the justifying metric changes to fit the
conclusion, that is not a framework — it is rationalisation with a rotating
alibi. The research manager's "opposite trades" formulation is elegant, and
elegant is exactly what a rationalisation sounds like.

On the substance: Rule of 40 of 34, decelerating from 17% to 14.6%, is the
profile of a company whose multiple compresses rather than expands. Multiple
compression on a 26.8x forward earner is a long way down.

On the data: the source supplying our moving averages also claimed "strong
support around $95–100" on a stock trading at $89.20. That source does not know
what the word support means, and we are placing a stop using its arithmetic.
The two-averages-converging defence is a rationalisation of the same kind.

And the omission that should stop the trade outright: **net revenue retention is
missing**. For a subscription business, buying without it is buying without
knowing whether the base is growing or leaking. The record $100k+ additions are
being read as expansion because nobody checked.

### Neutral
The conservative officer's point about the rotating metric is the serious one,
and the desk should answer it rather than deflect. The metric did change. The
defence — that IOT and ESTC are opposite trades, quality-despite-price versus
price-despite-quality — is legitimate as a *statement of what is being bought*,
but it is not a framework, and the officer is right that it can be made to fit
anything after the fact. What would make it a framework is stating the rule in
advance: **a premium above the cohort median requires a quality justification; a
discount to it requires only that quality not be deteriorating.** That rule
generates both ratings, and it also generates the condition on this one — which
is why NRR matters more than any multiple in this report.

On the streak: three Overweights after three Holds, and the previous run already
logged that the desk appeared to be correcting toward action after being told it
was too passive. The correction may have overshot. The specific discomfort here
is that **ESTC is the weakest of the three on business quality** — Rule of 40 of
34 against IOT's 51, decelerating growth against TRU's raised EPS guide — and is
receiving the same rating. That is recorded rather than argued away, and it is
why the rating is labelled the weakest of the three rather than presented as
equivalent.

Three things the proposal assumes that could be false:

First, **that NRR is healthy.** It is unsourced, it is directly obtainable from
the filings, and it determines whether the headline customer additions mean what
the bull says. The desk should have fetched it instead of debating around it —

Second, that the $83.60–$84.25 cluster is real support, when the same source
mislabelled resistance as support on the same page.

Third, that "the de-rating already happened." The bear's crash-and-recover point
is correct and under-weighted: down 11% over 52 weeks but up 112% from the low
